# Investment Approach — Part 1 "Specialness" Test (summary)
Source: Notion page "Investment_Approach_and_Process" (last edited 2026-05-19), read 2026-10-19.

**Core question:** Would I want to own this whole business for a long time? → What makes it *special*?

**"Special" = some combination of:**
1. Clear secular growth drivers
2. A widening moat
3. Excellent management (incl. "sidecar" operators — Hunter Harrison, Jamie Dimon, Buffett type)
4. A very clear, compelling customer value proposition

**Flexible routes to special:** exceptional capital allocation; an exceptional leader; building something exceptional inside a secular growth area; true oligopolies (railroads, industrial gases).

**Price vs quality:** price must be appropriate, but *bargains that are not special are a "no"* for this concentrated book (express those via ETFs/index). ROI on research time is lower for non-special businesses.

**Output of the filter:** two buckets — **super special (act)** or **just good (track for dislocations)**. Aim: find the Costcos/Amazons (Nick Sleep: a handful of positions that take over the portfolio).

## How it is applied in this screen
- Stage 3 triage: a cheap-but-ordinary business (no moat, no secular driver, no exceptional management) is marked NO even if it passes the Buffett tests mechanically ("bargain without specialness").
- Stage 4: each KEEP gets a one-line **Specialness** verdict: *Special / Good-track-for-dislocation / Not special*, citing which of the four attributes (if any) apply.
