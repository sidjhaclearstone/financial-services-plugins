## 3. What the market is doubting (why <18x)
SNA has historically traded at roughly 14–18x forward EPS (estimate, not sourced in our data), so 17.4x is **not a dislocation**. It is roughly the stock's normal multiple, and it qualified only via the P/E route: the NTM consensus FCF yield of 2.9% fails, apparently because forecasts include finance-receivable growth. The doubts:
(a) **Tools Group stagnation.** Technicians are "cash rich but confidence poor" and avoid long-payback items such as tool storage (CEO, Q2-25 and Q4-25 calls).
(b) **Earnings flat for three years** with the margin near its peak.
(c) **Credit exposure.** The technician/franchisee loan book is exposed if confidence weakens further.
(d) **Tariffs and material costs**, plus a weak Europe.
(e) **Long-term EV/ADAS mix** (fewer mechanical repairs, more diagnostics).

Our view: (a) is **partly temporary**, since tool storage turned positive and franchisee sell-through is beating sell-in (StreetAccount 6-May-2026). (b) is **right**: absent margin expansion this is a 5–7% grower. The market is not wrong.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS ≥ +7% in each of FY2026–FY2030)
FY1 2026 consensus is +2.9%, and even the high estimate ($20.03) is only +4.4% (FactSet). P(FY1 ≥7%) ≈ 0. History: 6/10 years ≥7%, with 2020 −5.1% and 2025 −1.6% (FactSet). **P(Test 3) ≈ 1%**. FAIL.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is whether Tools Group demand recovers to its ~4% organic target once technician confidence returns, and whether the 25–26% margin holds against tariffs. Two years of ≥4% Tools organic growth with margin intact would lift P(Test 2) to ~75%. Rising credit delinquencies, or margins back below 24%, would cut it to ~55%.
