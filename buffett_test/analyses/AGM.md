## 3. What the market is doubting
At 8.8x, the market applies a GSE/financial discount. The concerns are (a) structural leverage of ~30x net debt/EBITDA and political/charter risk, (b) the agricultural credit cycle (commodity prices, input costs; mgmt says Q2 provisioning was "too early" to call, May-2026), (c) concentration in data-centre and broadband infrastructure loans, and (d) thin coverage (3 analysts). The doubt is **mostly temporary or overdone**. In 10 years the worst EPS year was −0.1% (2024), credit losses have stayed minimal, and the funding advantage comes from the charter. The charter risk is low-probability but **real** and cannot be diversified.

## 4. TEST 2 — P(EPS FY1+5 ≥ 1.30 × FY1 consensus)
Bar: 1.30 × $20.70 = **$26.91** in FY2031. FY3 consensus is $24.88, so FY3–FY6 needs only ~2.6%/yr. Starting from an ITC-inflated FY1, however, the bar is effectively a ~6.5%/yr underlying CAGR (estimate).
| Threat | P | Impact | Fails? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Farm-credit downturn (low commodity prices, high input costs) raises provisions | 15% | −15% | Mostly fail |
| ITC/tax-credit benefits shrink or disappear (policy change) | 15% | −8% to −10% | ~half fail |
| Spread compression or higher funding costs; GSE/charter policy shock | 5% | −20%+ | Fail |
| Capital constraints slow volume growth (needs preferred/common issuance) | 10% | −5% | ~third fail |

Arithmetic: P(fail) ≈ 12% + 7% + 5% + 3% − overlap ~5% ≈ **22%**. **P(Test2) ≈ 78% → FAIL.** A low-loss history supports this, but leverage, tax-credit and charter exposure keep it under the 80% anchor.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS ≥ +7% in each of next 5 years)
- History: 8/10 years ≥7% (2020 +7.2%, 2024 −0.1%, 2025 +6.5%). Both of the last two years missed.
- FY1 +24% is well covered (P ≈ 0.95). FY2 +11.5% (P ≈ 0.75). FY3 +7.8% (n=2) has a narrow margin, about 0.55. FY4–5 are each ~0.6 because of credit and ITC lumpiness.
- P ≈ 0.95 × 0.75 × 0.55 × 0.6 × 0.6 ≈ **14%**. Banks/insurers rarely exceed 25%. **P(Test3) ≈ 14% → FAIL.**

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is the sustainability of core earnings growth ex-tax credits, together with credit quality through an ag downturn. Evidence of 10%+ core EPS growth ex-ITC in FY2027 with stable non-accruals would lift Test 2 toward 85%. A loss of renewable ITC benefits or a GSE reform proposal would cut it toward 65%. Low confidence: n=3 analysts, and the decomposition is estimated.

## 7. Earnings quality
FCF conversion is **not meaningful** (financial; FY2025 FCF −$9m reflects loan purchases). The core vs GAAP gap is small (−3.5% 5y avg, +0.3% latest; FactSet). GAAP includes fair-value hedge swings, which mostly net out. Watch item: tax credits flowing through EPS. No flag beyond that.
