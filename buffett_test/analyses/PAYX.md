## 3. What the market is doubting (why FCF yield ≥6% / ~18x)
For most of the past decade PAYX traded in the high-20s P/E. At 18.1x with a 6.5% FCF yield, the market doubts:
(a) **AI disruption.** Both AI-native HCM entrants and AI cutting SMB white-collar headcount ("AI concerns", "AI disintermediation" in every broker note, StreetAccount 26-Mar-2026).
(b) **Slow organic growth.** The "low-hire" labour market keeps organic growth at ~5–6%, versus a market that had priced in double digits.
(c) **Falling float income** as the Fed cuts.
(d) **The Paycor deal.** It lowered ROIC (37.6%→20.6%, FactSet) and added leverage.

Our view: (a) is **largely wrong** on a five-year view. Payroll/tax filing is regulated and sticky, Paychex is already monetising its own AI (WISE), and the shares fell ~8% on a stable Q1 (StreetAccount 23-Sep-2026, $106.50), which looks like sentiment. (b) and (c) are **right**: this is now a ~7% EPS grower, so a lower multiple is justified. The cheapness is mostly the multiple resetting, not a temporary earnings dip.

## 4. TEST 2 — P(EPS label 2031 ≥ 1.30 × FY1 = $7.75)
FY3 consensus is $6.87 (FactSet), so the last three years need ~4.1%/yr. What would make EPS lower?

| Threat | Prob. (5y) | EPS impact | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growth stalls below the 5.4% bar (organic ~4%, no leverage) | 15% | fails ~half | ~7% |
| Recession at endpoint: fewer checks/clients and rates cut toward zero (double hit) | 15% | −5 to −10% | ~5% |
| AI/competitive price compression in SMB payroll (Gusto, Rippling, ADP, embedded payroll) | 12% | −5 to −10% | ~5% |
| Float yield falls to ~2% (partial, non-recession) | 25% | −2 to −4% | ~3% |

Σ ≈ 20%, less ~3 pts overlap → failure ≈ 17%. Because the driver stack sits only 0.3 pt above the bar, we round down: **P(Test 2) ≈ 80%**. FAIL. This matches GEHC/BLKB (80%) in calibration: a good business whose base case only just clears the bar.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS ≥ +7% in each of the next 5 fiscal years)
FY1 is +8.2% (threshold $5.90 vs mean $5.96, std $0.027; three quarters still to report): P ≈ 0.8. FY2 (+7.5%) and FY3 (+7.2%) sit right on the line: P ≈ 0.6 each. FY4–5: P ≈ 0.55 each. History: 7/10 years ≥7% (misses: label 2019 +5.6%, 2020 +1.3%, 2024 +5.5%; FactSet). The COVID year nearly halted growth.
- No recession: 0.55 × 0.8 × 0.6 × 0.6 × 0.55 × 0.55 ≈ 0.048
- Recession branch adds ~0.01
- **P(Test 3) ≈ 6%**. FAIL.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is whether organic growth re-accelerates to ≥6–7% with Paycor cross-sell, or settles at 4–5%. Two years of ≥6% organic growth with stable retention, plus buybacks stepping up once leverage falls below 1x, would lift P(Test 2) to ~85%. Evidence of AI-driven price compression in the sub-50-employee segment would cut it to ~70%.

## 7. Earnings quality
The adj-vs-GAAP gap is 4.0% 5y avg and 12.7% latest (Paycor intangible amortisation and integration costs; FactSet). That is below 15%, so no flag, but watch it. FCF conversion is 1.08x 10y / 1.06x 5y (FactSet). Note that ~5–10% of operating income is interest on client funds (estimate), which is rate-sensitive.
