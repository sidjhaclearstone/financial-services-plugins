# Bright Horizons Family Solutions, Inc. (BFAM-US) — Buffett driver analysis
As-of 2026-10-19 | NTM P/E 12.1 (FactSet, 19-Oct-2026) | Mkt cap $3.4bn (FMP, 19-Oct-2026; price $69.52 FactSet close) | RBICS Household Services / Personal Professional Services / Educational Services | Confidence: Medium
Specialness (PM Part 1 test): **Good–track for dislocation**. Two attributes apply. One is a compelling customer value proposition: the employer-sponsored model, in which corporate clients fund the capacity and back-up care has <10% penetration of existing clients. The other is a secular driver: employer care benefits. The full-service centre estate, however, earns mediocre returns and has been shrinking.

Data caveat: FactSet did not return FCF, ROIC, ROE or share count for BFAM. Shares are derived from net income / diluted EPS, about 57.4m in FY2025. The FMP market cap implies ~48.6m shares, which looks low, so the cap may be understated (≈$4.0bn on 57m shares, estimate). Confidence is reduced accordingly.

## 3. What the market is doubting
The shares fell from $81.83 (Feb-2026) to $69.52 (FactSet/StreetAccount). The market doubts four things:
- the full-service centre business: Australia oversupply forced FY2026 full-service margin flat (FactSet transcript, May/Jul-2026);
- structural demand for office-adjacent care in a hybrid-work world with falling birth rates;
- labour-cost pass-through;
- the high adjusted-to-GAAP gap.

The doubt is **partly right**: occupancy is still mid/high-60s vs a ~70% target, and the centre estate is being shrunk. It is **partly wrong** on back-up care, a high-margin, asset-light, double-digit grower that is now the main profit engine.

## 4. TEST 2 — P(EPS FY2031 ≥ 1.30 × FY1 consensus)
Bar: 1.30 × $5.11 = **$6.65** (FactSet consensus FY1 2026). FY3 consensus is already $6.90. Base rate is weak: only 1 of 6 rolling 5-yr windows since FY2015 achieved ≥1.3x (FactSet actuals), but that is COVID-driven.

Survival = 0.93 × 0.94 × 0.96 × 0.97 × 0.96 = 0.78. The poor historical base rate trims this, so **P ≈ 76% → FAIL** (one structural threat plus cyclical and pandemic exposure).

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS +7% in every one of the next 5 years)
- Recurring revenue: high in the sense of annual contracts and enrolled children. Volume, however, is employment-sensitive, and back-up care is a discretionary employer benefit.
- Pricing power: tuition rises ~4% a year.
- Weak year: buybacks are debt-financed and would likely pause.
- History: 9/10 years ≥7% (FactSet actuals). The miss was 2020 at −58%, so the line is anything but recession-proof.

P(no recession) 0.60 × 0.82^5 (0.37) = 0.22. Add P(recession) 0.40 × P(still ≥7% in that year) 0.30 × 0.82^4 (0.45) = 0.05. Total **~27% → FAIL**.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is whether full-service margins return to the ~10% historical level as underperforming centres close. Two developments would lift Test 2 to ~85%: back-up care sustaining 12%+ growth, and full-service occupancy reaching ~70% with net centre openings in 2027. Enrollment declining outside Australia, or back-up care growth falling below 8%, would cut it to ~65%.

## 7. Earnings quality
**Flag: ADJ_GAAP_GAP.** Adjusted EPS of $4.55 is 35% above GAAP EPS of $3.36 in FY2025, and the 5-yr average gap is 73% (FactSet). Add-backs are amortisation of acquired intangibles, SBC, and recurring impairment/closure costs. The closure costs are effectively recurring while the estate is rationalised, so GAAP is the more honest base. FCF conversion cannot be assessed because FCF is missing from the FactSet pull.
