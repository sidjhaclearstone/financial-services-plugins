# T-Mobile US, Inc. (TMUS-US) — Buffett driver analysis
As-of 2026-10-19 | NTM P/E 11.7 (FactSet, 19-Oct-2026) | Mkt cap $167.4bn (FMP, 19-Oct-2026; price $156.09 FactSet close) | RBICS Telecommunications / Wireless and Wireline Telecommunications Services / Wireless Services | Confidence: Medium
Specialness (PM Part 1 test): **Special**. Four attributes apply:
- **Oligopoly:** a member of the 3-player US wireless oligopoly.
- **Widening moat:** the 5G mid-band network lead from Sprint spectrum, plus consistent share gains.
- **Excellent management and capital allocation:** Sprint integration, synergy delivery, and large buybacks.
- **Compelling customer value proposition:** best network plus value pricing.

It is tempered by leverage and an intensifying price war.

## 3. What the market is doubting
Shares are down from $247 (Jul-2025) to $156 (FactSet/StreetAccount price tags). The market doubts five things:
- intensified wireless competition: Verizon and AT&T promotions, and fibre-plus-wireless convergence bundles;
- cable MVNOs taking share;
- satellite direct-to-device disruption, flagged in Q2-2026 coverage;
- rising churn;
- management dropping postpaid-phone net-add disclosure (FactSet StreetAccount, Feb/Jul-2026).

KeyBanc argues TMUS is structurally disadvantaged by lack of fibre. The doubt is **partly right**: industry growth is slowing, and share gains are being bought with promotions. It is **largely wrong as a 5-yr earnings call**. This is a rational 3-player oligopoly with mgmt explicitly de-emphasising device promotions, and FCF is still compounding (FactSet FCF $2.8bn in 2022 → $11.3bn in 2025). Several brokers set targets near 15x FCF (FactSet StreetAccount, Feb-2026).

## 4. TEST 2 — P(EPS FY2031 ≥ 1.30 × FY1 consensus)
Bar: 1.30 × $10.92 = **$14.20** (FactSet consensus FY1 2026). FY2 consensus of $13.98 is almost there, and FY3 is $16.79. On the driver stack alone (~11%/yr), FY2031 EPS is ~$18. Base rate: 5 of 6 rolling 5-yr windows since FY2015 achieved ≥1.3x (FactSet actuals).

| Threat | P | EPS impact | P(causes fail) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price war (cable MVNOs, Verizon/AT&T convergence bundles); ARPA flat | 30% | −10–20% vs path | 10% |
| Satellite D2D / new spectrum entrant erodes rural/value segment | 10% | −10% | 4% |
| Leverage: refinancing at higher rates, buybacks curtailed | 15% | −5–10% | 3% |
| Capex/spectrum step-up (fibre JVs, 6G) lowers FCF and buybacks | 15% | −5–10% | 3% |

Survival = 0.90 × 0.96 × 0.97 × 0.97 = 0.81. Demand is recurring and non-cyclical, and the consensus cushion is wide, which nudges this up. **P ≈ 82% → FAIL**: the competitive threat is credible and the balance sheet carries 3.6x leverage.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS +7% in every one of the next 5 years)
- Recurring revenue: ~90%+ subscription service revenue (estimate). Recession sensitivity is low (wireless is a utility-like spend), so pricing power has been demonstrated.
- History: 6/10 years ≥7% (FactSet actuals). The misses were 2020–22 (Sprint integration costs) and 2025 (+0.6%, a normal year with heavy promotion and integration).
- A recession is not the main risk. The risk is a competitive or one-off year (merger costs, spectrum write-downs).

P(each year ≥7%) ≈ 0.78, so 0.78^5 = 0.29. Times 0.95 for recession gives **~27% → FAIL**.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is whether the US wireless industry stays rational on price as growth slows and cable and satellite push in. Stabilising churn plus ARPA growth ≥3% through 2027 would lift Test 2 to ~88%. A sustained industry price cut, ARPA down year-on-year, would cut it to ~65%.

## 7. Earnings quality
Adj = GAAP (5-yr gap 0.0%; FactSet). FCF conversion is 0.69 (5y) and 0.26 (10y) (FactSet), depressed by the 2019–22 5G/Sprint integration capex peak (capex $21.7bn in 2021).
