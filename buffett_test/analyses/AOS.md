## 3. What the market is doubting
EPS has been flat since 2023 ($3.81 → $3.73 → $3.85, FactSet), and consensus FY1 2026 is −3.5%. Margins sit at the 91st percentile of their own history. The doubts:
- Weak residential new construction.
- Tougher wholesale competition.
- China: sales down ~10% in 2025 and the subsidy programme discontinued (Q3-25 call). The China review is ongoing "with all options open" (Q2-26 call, StreetAccount).
- Tariffs and steel costs.
The share price went from $72.04 (Jan-2026) to $58.68 (FactSet close). The doubt is **mostly temporary** for the North American core, where replacement demand is roughly 80%+ of units (general industry knowledge, estimate). It is **right** that structural growth is only ~4–6%.

## 4. TEST 2 — P(EPS FY2031 ≥ 1.30 × FY1 = $4.83)
FY1 $3.72 → $4.83 needs 5.4%/yr. FY3 consensus is $4.37, so 2028–31 needs only 3.4%/yr (FactSet consensus). The driver stack of ~6% clears 5.4% by just 0.6 pts.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS grows ≥7% in every one of the next 5 years)
- FY1 2026 consensus −3.5% (high $3.77 is still −2%) (FactSet consensus), so year one fails almost certainly.
- History: only 5/10 years ≥7%, with declines in 2019, 2020 and 2024.
- About 80% of demand is replacement, but EPS still swings with price-cost and new-construction volumes.
**≈1% → FAIL.**

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key question is whether North American volumes and pricing re-accelerate in 2027–28, as consensus's +8.4%/yr requires, or whether 2023–26 margins were a peak. Evidence of durable share gains with a credible heat-pump/tankless line-up, plus a value-accretive China exit, would lift Test 2 toward ~80%.
