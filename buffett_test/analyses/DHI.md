## 3. What the market is doubting
At 11.9x NTM, the market doubts that FY2026 is a trough. Points in the evidence: FY26 closings guidance was reduced, cancellations are rising and unsold completed homes are above seasonal levels (Wells Fargo, Truist; StreetAccount 22-Jul-2026); gross margin had not troughed in January (Oppenheimer, Jan-26); RBC (Underperform) flags FY27 demand and inflation risk; and existing-home supply is rising as sellers capitulate (StreetAccount, 11-Oct-2026). Against that, Goldman sees GM upside from record-low cycle times and cost deflation. My view: the doubt is **right about the cycle** (EPS fell 16% in FY23 and 19% in FY25, with -10% expected in FY26) but **probably too harsh on the five-year view**. Margins are already back to mid-cycle, the balance sheet is strong, and buybacks of roughly 6–11% of market cap a year compound per-share EPS.

## 4. TEST 2 — P(EPS FY2031 ≥ 1.30 × 10.45 = $13.58)
Consensus FY3 (FY28) is already $13.11. With buybacks shrinking the count about 5%/yr, a mid-cycle FY2031 would pass easily ($13.11 × 1.04^3 ≈ $14.7, estimate). What would put EPS below $13.58 in FY2031?
- (a) A housing downturn in FY2030–31 (rate spike, recession, credit event): P 22%, EPS -30–50%.
- (b) Structural margin compression (persistent incentives, recovering existing-home supply, land inflation) holding EBIT margin ≤11%: P 15%, EPS -20%.
- (c) Volume stagnation with mortgage rates higher for longer: P 8%.
- (d) Buyback pace cut sharply (cash redeployed to land): P 3%.
Σ = 48%, less ~10 pts overlap between (a)/(b)/(c) = **38% failure → P(Test2) ≈ 62%**. FAIL. The single-cycle exposure caps it well below 80%, consistent with the calibration anchors.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS ≥ +7% in each of FY2026–FY2030)
FY1 (Sep-2026, already ended, results due late October) consensus is -9.7%, with a range of -11.5% to -8.6% and std $0.09, so P(FY1 ≥ 7%) ≈ 0. History: 7/10 years ≥7%, with declines in FY23 (-16.3%) and FY25 (-19.3%) (FactSet). There is no recurring revenue: each home is a one-off sale that depends on mortgage affordability. Even excluding FY1, P(no downturn in 4 years) ~0.5 × P(normal year ≥7%)^4 ~0.75^4 gives ≈ 16%. **P(Test3) ≈ 1%**. FAIL.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is the mortgage-rate and affordability path: whether incentives can normalise before existing-home supply competes away new-home pricing power. Things that would raise the view: gross margin stabilising with incentives falling over the next two to three quarters, FY27 closings growth guided at ≥5%, and continued buybacks of ≥$2.5bn a year (estimate). Things that would lower it: FY27 consensus moving toward the $9.18 low estimate, or margins below the FY15–19 band (~11–12%).

## 7. Earnings quality
Adj EPS equals GAAP (gap 0.0%, FactSet), so no adjustment issue. **Flag: FCF conversion 0.43 (10y) / 0.44 (5y)** (FactSet). The shortfall is almost entirely inventory (land and homes under construction) built to fund growth; FY21–22 FCF was ~$0 on record earnings. When growth slows, conversion rises (FY23 80%, FY25 78% of NI, FactSet). The quality is acceptable, but reported earnings overstate distributable cash in growth years.
