## 3. What the market is doubting (why <18x)
The stock is $316.41, down from $360.97 in Feb-2026 (StreetAccount). It fell 20.30 on the Q2-26 print. At 16.1x the market prices in four things:
- **Consumer-credit and T&E cyclicality.** EPS fell 52.8% in 2020 (FactSet).
- **A rewards arms race.** Premium-card competition is increasing VCE costs to 44% of revenue (mgmt, Feb-2026).
- **Reinvestment of upside** into marketing and tech, which limits EPS beats. Loss of the Lowe's commercial and Amazon small-business programmes adds a modest headwind (Truist and Monness, Apr-2026).
- **Regulatory noise.** This includes the Credit Card Competition Act, which management calls "net neutral" (Feb-2026).

Assessment: **mostly temporary or overdone on competition.** Attrition is "low and stable even after fee increases" (Jul-2026). The Platinum refresh outperformed and the customer base is skewing younger. The discount is **right in principle on cyclicality**. A spend-centric lender to affluent customers is less cyclical than a bank, but its EPS still breaks in a deep recession or a T&E shock.

## 4. TEST 2 — P(EPS FY2031 ≥ 1.30 × FY1 consensus)
The bar is 1.30 × $17.68 = **$22.99**. FY3 (2028) consensus is already $23.04 (low $21.60). A 10%/yr path from FY3 gives $30.7, a 33% cushion. Failure requires roughly a 25–30% EPS drop near the endpoint.
| Threat | P | EPS impact | Weight that fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| Endpoint recession with credit losses and lower T&E (2009, 2020: −40 to −50%) | 15% | −25 to −50% | 0.6 → 9.0 |
| Premium-card competition forces rewards and fee concessions | 10% | −10 to −15% | 0.35 → 3.5 |
| Regulation (interchange caps, CCCA, APR caps) | 6% | −10% | 0.35 → 2.1 |
| Underwriting drift (Pay Over Time, younger cohorts) | 5% | −10 to −15% | 0.3 → 1.5 |
| Shock to travel spend (pandemic or geopolitical) | 3% | −30% | 0.5 → 1.5 |

Arithmetic: Σ ≈ 17.6% − ~2 overlap + ~2 unknowns ≈ 18% ⇒ P ≈ 82%. Cyclical names are held below 80%, in line with BKNG (78%) for travel spend and above banks (JPM 65%, CFR 75%), so it is set at **P(Test2) ≈ 78% → FAIL.**

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS ≥ +7% in each of next 5 years)
- History: **5/10** years at ≥7% (FactSet). The misses were 2016 +5.0%, 2017 +3.9%, 2019 +1.0%, 2020 −52.8% and 2022 −1.7%. The calibration anchor for financials is ≤25%.
- FY1: +15.0%, low +13.1% ⇒ 0.98. FY2: +13.7%, low +8.9% ⇒ 0.92. Normal later years: 0.85 each.
- P(no recession in years 3–5) 0.55. In a recession year, P(≥7%) is 0.05: provisions rise and billings drop.
- 0.98 × 0.92 × 0.85² × (0.55 × 0.85 + 0.45 × 0.05) = 0.98 × 0.92 × 0.7225 × 0.49 ≈ 0.32. Haircut ×0.6 for reinvestment and credit-build years, following the 5/10 history ⇒ **≈ 18% → FAIL.**

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is how far credit and T&E would fall in the next recession. The customer base is more affluent and more spend-centric than in 2009, but lending balances are larger. A mild recession in which EPS falls less than 10% would lift Test 2 toward 85%. Two things would cut it toward 70%: a rewards war that drives VCE above 46% of revenue, or interchange regulation.
