## 3. What the market is doubting
At 12.2x NTM, the market doubts three things:
- **Whether the P&C cycle has peaked.** Property rates are already declining, and the 2026 Specialty combined-ratio guide is 93.2%, worse than the long-run ambition.
- **Long-haul trucking / commercial auto reserve adequacy**, an industry-wide sore spot.
- **Title earnings**, which depend on a housing recovery.

Assessment: the doubt is **largely right for the next 2–3 years**. Consensus itself shows earnings falling in 2026 and growing slowly after. It is **wrong only on franchise durability**: reserving is conservative, and capital return is strong (buyback plus special dividend yields of ~10% in 2024–25, FactSet cash-flow data).

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS grows ≥ 7% in EVERY one of the next 5 years)
- **History:** EPS grew ≥7% in 6 of the last 10 years (FactSet), with declines in 2019, 2022 and 2023.
- **FY1:** FY2026 consensus is −6.1%, with three quarters reported. P(FY1 ≥7%) ≈ 3%.
- **Later years:** P&C earnings are cycle-driven, and title is volume-sensitive to housing.

Overall **≈2% → FAIL**.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is where the specialty P&C cycle goes next: whether commercial auto rate increases stay ahead of loss trend while property and casualty pricing softens. The cycle sets the underwriting-margin leg.
- **Upgrade:** Specialty combined ratio sustained ≤92% through 2027 with continued favourable development, and a title recovery.
- **Downgrade:** reserve strengthening in trucking, or a combined ratio above 96%.

## 7. Earnings quality
The latest GAAP EPS of 3.72 is **15.4% above** adjusted EPS of 3.15 (FactSet). The gap comes from realised and unrealised investment gains. It flatters GAAP, so adjusted is the cleaner and more conservative basis; the 5-year average gap is −2.5%. FCF conversion is not meaningful for an insurer.
