Specialness (PM Part 1 test): **Good–track for dislocation** — a compelling customer value proposition (the default rural-lifestyle store for "Life Out Here" customers, Neighbor's Club loyalty, needs-based consumable/usable/edible mix) with a long record of excellent capital allocation. But EPS has been flat for four years, competition in pet is intense, and there is no clear secular growth driver, so the moat is not demonstrably widening.

## 3. What the market is doubting (why it trades below 18x)
TSCO has historically traded at roughly 20–25x (estimate) and now trades at 16.2x NTM. The shares fell from $59.62 (Jul-2025) to $51.83 (Jan-2026) and then to $29.36 after the Q2-2026 report. They are $32.81 today (StreetAccount/FactSet prices). There are three reasons for the de-rating:
- **A growth algorithm that has stalled.** FY2026 guidance was cut from EPS $2.13–2.23 with comps of +1–3% (Jan-2026) to EPS $1.90–2.00, comps of −1% to flat and an adjusted operating margin of 8.5–8.8% (Q2-26 call, Jul-2026). This is the fourth flat EPS year in a row.
- **Weak end markets.** Management says farm and ranch, pet and home maintenance (~80% of the addressable market) are flat to negative (Sep-2026). Pet faces Chewy, Walmart and Amazon, and management has made reaccelerating pet a priority.
- **Margin.** Freight and fuel costs are high, and tariff refunds are being reinvested in price on traffic-driving consumables. The 10–10.5% operating-margin target from the Dec-2024 Analyst Day now looks distant. Mizuho downgraded the stock to neutral with a $32 target (Jul-2026), and management will reset its long-term framework with Q4-2026 results.

Assessment: **partly temporary, partly right.** The needs-based core ("our needs-based businesses continue to perform well", Q2-26 release), loyalty and store growth support a return to mid-single-digit EPS growth. The market is right, though, that the pre-2022 13% compounding is unlikely to come back: comps are stuck below the ~2% leverage point, margin is under pressure from reinvestment, and buybacks are constrained.

## 4. TEST 2 — P(EPS FY2031 ≥ 1.30 × FY1 consensus)
The bar is 1.30 × $1.938 = **$2.52**, which needs 5.4%/yr from a depressed FY1. FY3 consensus is $2.25, so FY3→FY6 still needs 3.8%/yr.

| Threat | P | Impact | Fails? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structural comp stagnation (store saturation, pet share loss), comps 0–1% | 25% | EPS ~3–4%/yr | ~half → 12.5% |
| Gross/operating margin resets to ~8% (permanent price reinvestment, freight) | 15% | −10 to −15% | ~0.6 → 9% |
| Endpoint recession / farm downturn in FY2030–31 | 15% | −5 to −10% (needs-based mix cushions it) | ~0.3 → 4.5% |
| Balance-sheet constraint: 2.9x incl. leases, payouts above FCF, so buybacks fall | 10% | −1 to −1.5 pts/yr | overlaps; ~3% |

Arithmetic: 12.5 + 9 + 4.5 + 3 = 29%, less ~4 pts of overlap (the margin and stagnation threats are correlated) ≈ 25% fail. **P(Test2) ≈ 75% → FAIL.** This is a good business with a credible structural growth threat, so it sits at the POOL level (75%). It is less cyclical than POOL but has a weaker driver cushion.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is whether comps return above ~2% (management's leverage point) once farm and ranch laps in Q4-2026 and pet stabilises, or whether TSCO is now a ~2% comp, ~9% margin retailer. Two things would lift Test 2 toward 82–85%: positive comps through 2027 with margin back above 9.5%, and a credible long-term framework at Q4-2026 results. Continued share loss in pet, or buybacks shrinking because of leverage, would push it toward 65%.

## 7. Earnings quality
Adjusted EPS equals GAAP (gap 0.0%, FactSet), so the earnings themselves are clean. **Flag: FCF conversion is persistently below 80%**: 0.57 over 5y and 0.74 over 10y (CFO − capex vs net income, FactSet). The cause is growth capex (new stores, distribution centres, Project Fusion remodels, owned real estate), which ran at $754–895m a year in 2022–25 against D&A of ~$490m. It is not an accounting problem, but cash returns ($865m–1.07bn a year of buybacks plus dividends in 2023–25) exceeded FCF. Net debt rose from $4.07bn to $5.75bn (FactSet, including leases).
