# Adobe Inc. (ADBE-US) — Buffett driver analysis
As-of 2026-10-19 | NTM P/E 9.0 (FactSet, 19-Oct-2026) | Mkt cap $95.4bn (FactSet GlobalPrices, 16-Oct-2026) | RBICS Software / Design and Engineering Software | Confidence: Medium
Specialness (PM Part 1 test): **Good–track for dislocation** — capital allocation is excellent (buybacks of ~100% of FCF at a ~10% FCF yield) and the customer value proposition is compelling (Creative Cloud, Acrobat/PDF standard, Firefly). But the moat is **no longer demonstrably widening**: net new ARR fell 38% y/y in Q3 FY26, and Canva, Figma and genAI-native tools are taking the low end. That leaves Adobe short of "super special" until genAI monetisation is proven.

## 3. What the market is doubting
At 9.0x, the market prices Adobe as a possible genAI casualty. The concerns are (a) generative image/video models and Canva/Figma commoditising creative seats, (b) LLMs eroding Acrobat's PDF franchise, (c) decelerating net new ARR (−38% y/y in Q3 FY26), with RPO and cRPO falling sequentially (StreetAccount, Sep-2026), and (d) leadership change: CEO Narayen stepped down and Anil Chakravarthy took over, with CFO and Creative-head hires pending. The doubt is **partly right** on growth: the subscription base is maturing and the low-end/prosumer segment is under pressure. It looks **too severe** on earnings. AI-first ARR is above $650M, about 95% of revenue is recurring subscription (company disclosure, approximate), the net cash position is neutral, and a 10% FCF yield means EPS can compound even if revenue merely grows low single digits.

## 4. TEST 2 — P(EPS FY1+5 ≥ 1.30 × FY1 consensus)
Bar: 1.30 × $24.48 = **$31.82** in FY2031. FY3 consensus is already $31.42 (n=18; low $27.49), so FY3–FY6 needs only ~0.4%/yr. Buyback math: with ~5%/yr share shrink, EPS reaches the bar provided non-GAAP net income does not fall below the FY26 level (~$10.2bn, estimate) by FY2031.
| Threat | P | Impact | Fails? |
|---|---|---|---|
| GenAI commoditisation: creative seat ARPU and seat counts fall, revenue flat or down, margin forced to ~30% | 12% | Net income −15% to −25% | Yes |
| Acrobat/Document Cloud disrupted by LLM-native document tools | 8% | −10% | ~half fail (overlaps with the above) |
| Execution or strategy misstep under the new CEO (large dilutive AI acquisition, slower buybacks) | 8% | −5% to −10% | ~third fail |
| Recession (enterprise Digital Experience seat cuts) | 10% | −5% | rarely fails given the head start |

Arithmetic: P(fail) ≈ 12% + 4% + 2.5% + 1% − overlap ~4.5% ≈ **15%**. **P(Test2) ≈ 85% → FAIL (near-miss).** The business is a wide-moat-type compounder, but one credible disruption threat holds it at the top of the "1 credible threat" band.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS ≥ +7% in each of next 5 years)
- History: **10/10** years ≥7%, including FY2020 (+28.3%) and FY2022 (+9.9%, the worst year). Revenue is ~95% recurring, and pricing power has been demonstrated, though it is now contested.
- FY1 (Nov-2026, one quarter left): +16.9%, std $0.03, n=33, so P ≈ 0.98.
- FY2 +13.3% (low $26.00 = +6.2%) → P(≥7%) ≈ 0.85. FY3–FY5: buybacks of ~7 pts give a floor even if organic net income is flat, but genAI-driven price/margin reinvestment could produce a sub-7% year. Each year ≈ 0.75–0.80.
- In a recession year, the subscription model plus buybacks keep EPS growth likely positive. Hitting ≥7% depends on buyback pace.
- P ≈ 0.98 × 0.85 × 0.78 × 0.76 × 0.75 ≈ **37%**. **P(Test3) ≈ 37% → FAIL.**

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is whether genAI expands Adobe's market (consumption credits, Firefly, GenStudio for enterprises) or compresses it (seat deflation, prosumer churn to Canva and to free model-native tools). Net new ARR stabilising or growing in FY2027, with an operating margin of ~36% or better under the new CEO, would lift Test 2 to ~90% and Test 3 toward 45%. A second year of falling net new ARR plus margin cuts would push Test 2 toward 70%.

## 7. Earnings quality
**Flag (ADJ_GAAP_GAP).** The adjusted vs GAAP gap averaged 34.1% over 5y and was 25.4% in FY2025 (adj $20.94 vs GAAP $16.70; FactSet). It comes mainly from stock-based compensation (~$1.9bn/yr, estimate) and amortisation of acquired intangibles (Marketo, Workfront), plus the $1bn Figma termination fee in FY2024. FCF conversion is strong at 1.39 over 5y and 1.36 over 10y (deferred revenue and SBC add-back). SBC is a real cost that partly offsets the buyback (reflected in §2).
