## 3. What the market is doubting
Why 13.7x: (a) cyclicality of lending volumes in a higher-rate world (2023 adj EPS −6.9%, FactSet); (b) mortgage score/tri-merge economics under regulatory pressure and FICO's direct-licensing push (qualitative, widely reported 2025); (c) 2026 sector de-rating of data/information services on AI-commoditisation fears; (d) leverage and GAAP EPS at roughly half of adjusted. Our view: (a) is temporary—management says growth is now less tied to new-loan originations (FactSet StreetAccount, 15-Sep-2026) and volumes held up through the 2026 oil/Middle East shock (FactSet transcript, RBC conf. 11-Mar-2026); (b) is a real but bounded risk (mortgage is a minority of revenue, already depressed); (c) is largely wrong for a regulated credit-file owner—proprietary consumer credit data is an input AI needs, not one it replaces.

## 4. TEST 2 — P(FY2031 EPS ≥ 1.30 × FY1 = $6.28)
FY1 $4.83 × 1.30 = $6.28 — FY3 consensus is already $6.31 (FactSet), so the test needs only roughly flat EPS in 2029–31 after consensus. What would make 2031 EPS lower?

| Threat | Prob. (5y) | EPS impact | Contribution to failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deep credit recession in 2030–31 (late-window, not yet recovered) | 15% | −10 to −15% | ~5% |
| Regulatory hit to US mortgage/score pricing or CFPB action | 20% | −5 to −8% | ~3% |
| Major data breach / litigation | 5% | −10% | ~2% |
| AI/alternative-data share loss in analytics/fraud | 10% | −5 to −10% | ~3% |
| Execution/leverage (India, deal integration, refinancing) | 10% | −5% | ~2% |

Failure ≈ 5+3+2+3+2 = 15% (allowing overlap) → **P(Test 2) ≈ 85%**. FAIL, near-miss. A good business with credible cyclical and regulatory threats lands just short of the 90% anchor.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS +≥7% in every year FY2027–FY2031)
History: 8/10 years ≥7% (FactSet). 2020 passed (+7.5%), but 2022 (+5.2%) and 2023 (−6.9%) failed when originations fell under the rate shock. Recurring revenue is high (subscriptions, monitoring, fraud) but a large share is still transaction-based. Pricing power is solid. Buybacks are modest (~1.5 pts) and compete with deleveraging.
- P(no credit/origination shock in 5y) ≈ 50%; P(each normal year ≥7%) ≈ 0.85 → 0.5 × 0.85^5 = 0.22
- If a shock comes: P(that year still ≥7%) ≈ 0.20 → 0.5 × 0.20 × 0.85^4 = 0.05
- **P(Test 3) ≈ 27% → 25%**. FAIL.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key question is whether the US mortgage and score economics stay with the bureaus. If they do, and volumes outside mortgage keep growing mid-single-digit through a slowdown, TRU is a 90%+ Test-2 name. Evidence that would change the view: consistent organic growth ≥7% with EBIT margin rising back toward 24%, and leverage under 2.5x with buybacks resuming.

## 7. Earnings quality
**Flag.** Adj vs GAAP gap is 122% 5y avg (latest 86%) (FactSet), far above 15%. The gap comes mainly from amortisation of acquired intangibles (Callcredit, Neustar), a goodwill impairment in 2023 (GAAP EPS −$1.07, FactSet), and SBC. FCF conversion vs GAAP NI is 1.57x 10y / 1.83x 5y (FactSet), but against adjusted net income (4.30 × 196.6m ≈ $845m, computed) pre-dividend FCF of $662m is only ~78%. Heavy technology capex ($326m FY2025, FactSet) explains the shortfall. Treat adjusted EPS as somewhat flattering.
