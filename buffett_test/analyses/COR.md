Specialness (PM Part 1 test): **Good–track for dislocation**. It is a true oligopoly: one of three US drug wholesalers with MCK and CAH, which together handle the great majority of US pharmaceutical distribution. It also has a growing specialty/MSO franchise (OneOncology, RCA, EyeSouth). But the business earns a ~1% operating margin on a pass-through product, is exposed to policy and customer concentration (Walgreens), and its moat is stable rather than widening.

## 3. What the market is doubting — why 17x
The stock fell $50.77 (−17%) on 6-May-2026 when FY26 revenue guidance was cut (SA headline). The causes were IRA WAC reductions, oncology and grocery customer losses, faster brand-to-biosimilar conversion and slower GLP-1 growth (SA Q&A summary, 6-May-2026). Some Walgreens volume began moving away in July 2026 (Wells Fargo conf., 8-Sep-2026). Overlaying this are drug-pricing policy (MFN, IRA negotiation, ASP/340B changes), the fact that GLP-1 volume carries "modest" profitability (CFO, 8-Sep-2026), and MSO acquisitions that move Cencora into physician-practice risk.
Our view: the revenue doubts are mostly **wrong for EPS**. Operating income was resilient, and the low-margin revenue losses had "minimal impact on profitability" (SA, 6-May-2026). Walgreens is still described as a long-term partner. Policy risk is **real but historically survivable**: wholesalers have moved to fee-for-service contracts, and EPS grew in every year of 2015–25, including 2020 (+11.4%) and the opioid-settlement years. 17x is close to the group's normal range, so this is less a dislocation than a fair price for a reliable ~10% grower.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS +≥7% in every year FY2027–FY2031)
History: 9/10 years ≥7% (FactSet). The only miss was FY2017 (+4.6%, generic deflation). FY2020 grew 11.4%, and recession sensitivity is low. The risks to every-year growth are policy shocks and customer-loss years, not the macro cycle. Buybacks (~1.5 pts) and tuck-in M&A give mgmt levers to protect a weak year.
- FY2027: consensus +10.3%, std $0.18 → P(≥7%) ≈ 0.88
- P(each later year ≥7%) ≈ 0.82 (policy/customer-loss risk, no recession penalty)
- 0.88 × 0.82⁴ = 0.88 × 0.452 = 0.40; haircut ×0.85 for a correlated policy reset (MFN/IRA) that could hit two consecutive years → 0.34
- **P(Test 3) ≈ 33%**. FAIL, within the 20–40% compounder band.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is whether US drug-pricing reform (MFN, IRA negotiation scope, ASP rules) changes how wholesalers get paid. Evidence that would raise P(Test 2) toward 90% is new contract renewals on fee-for-service terms that are insensitive to WAC cuts, plus specialty/MSO operating income growing ≥10%. A negative change would be a policy that ties distributor fees to net rather than list prices, or a second large retail customer loss.

## 7. Earnings quality
**Flag.** The adj vs GAAP gap is 57.3% 5y avg (FactSet; latest ~101%), far above 15%. The gap comes from opioid litigation charges (GAAP EPS −$16.65 in FY2020, FactSet), LIFO, amortisation of acquired intangibles (Alliance Healthcare, OneOncology) and gains on equity stakes. Cash backs the adjusted figure: FY2025 pre-dividend FCF of $3.2bn (FactSet) vs adjusted net income of ~$3.1bn (16.00 × 195.2m, computed) ≈ 1.0x. The reported 2.55x 10y conversion vs depressed GAAP NI overstates this. Annual FCF swings with working capital. Opioid settlement payments (multi-year) are a real cash cost excluded from adjusted EPS.
