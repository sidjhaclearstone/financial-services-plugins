# Domino's Pizza (DPZ-US) — Buffett driver analysis
As-of 2026-10-19 | NTM P/E 15.0 (FactSet, 19-Oct-2026) | Mkt cap $10.2bn (FactSet, 19-Oct-2026) | Test 1 (v2) route: PE+FCF (pe_route=Y, fcf_route=Y) | FCF yield 6.0% (NTM consensus FCFPS $18.41 / price, FactSet; LTM 6.4%) | RBICS Hospitality Services / Restaurants and Bars | Confidence: Medium
Specialness (PM Part 1 test): **Good–track for dislocation**. Domino's has a very clear customer value proposition (the low-cost, fastest delivery/carryout pizza, built on scale in supply chain, technology and store density) and an asset-light franchise model with ~100% ROIC. But the delivery moat is narrowing as aggregators (DoorDash, Uber Eats) give independents the same reach, management execution is good rather than exceptional, and the international master-franchise layer is fragile (DPE).

## 3. What the market is doubting — why 15x (and a 6% FCF yield)
The shares fell from $396 after the Q4 FY25 print (SA, 23-Feb-2026) to $330 after Q1 FY26 (−$37.68 on the day) and $301 after Q3 FY26 (−$10.00) (SA headlines). They now trade at $305, 15.0x NTM.
- **US same-store sales are weak.** Order growth is "strong" but ticket is under pressure from fewer items per order, and the pressure is expected to persist through year-end (SA, 13-Oct-2026).
- **Unit growth was cut** because franchisee profitability is down y/y in absolute dollars, which weakens new-store openings (SA, 13-Oct-2026).
- **Aggregators.** Domino's is "not yet at fair share" on DoorDash/Uber Eats, while those platforms erode its delivery moat.
- **International fragility**, including the DPE (Australian master franchisee) turnaround.
- **Leverage** of 4.4x.
Our view: the near-term doubts (ticket, franchisee margins) are **temporary and cyclical**. Mgmt says all income cohorts grew in 2025 (SA, 23-Feb-2026), and pizza is a value category that tends to gain share in downturns. Competitor store closures continue to hand Domino's share. The structural doubt (aggregators equalising delivery) is **partly right**: it caps the growth algorithm at ~8% rather than the historic mid-teens. Leverage is manageable for a royalty stream (securitised, fixed-rate) but limits flexibility.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS +≥7% in every year FY2027–FY2031)
History: 8/10 years ≥7% (FactSet), including 2020 (+29.5%). The misses were 2022 (−7.9%, post-COVID normalisation and cost inflation) and 2025 (+5.3%). Royalty revenue is recurring and pizza is recession-resilient, but consensus growth sits just above 7% each year.
- FY2027: consensus +8.0%, std $0.39 (low $19.89 = +4.1%) → P(≥7%) ≈ 0.60
- P(each later year ≥7%) ≈ 0.60 (consensus +7.8% FY28 with one point of margin)
- 0.60 × 0.60⁴ = 0.078; ×1.25 for buyback flexibility to rescue a marginal year → 0.10
- **P(Test 3) ≈ 10%**. FAIL.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key question is whether franchisee profitability recovers enough to restore ~5% global unit growth while Domino's takes aggregator share without cannibalising its own delivery. Evidence that would raise P(Test 2) to ≥85%: US comps ≥3% with positive ticket, the US unit pipeline re-accelerating in 2027, and leverage held at ≤5x while buybacks continue. A negative change would be US delivery order counts turning negative.

## 7. Earnings quality
**No flag.** The adj vs GAAP gap is 0.1% 5y avg (FactSet). FCF conversion is 0.97x 10y / 0.98x 5y (FactSet). Equity is negative because buybacks were funded with securitised debt. That makes ROE meaningless and puts EPS growth partly on leverage: the share count fell 4.8%/yr over 10 years. Supply-chain revenue (food pass-through) inflates revenue and depresses EBIT margin optically.
