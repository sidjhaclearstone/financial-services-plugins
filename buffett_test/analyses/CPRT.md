Specialness (PM Part 1 test): **Special (moat under test)**. Copart is a salvage-auction duopoly with IAA (RB Global), with a widening moat in owned land (permitted yard capacity is very hard to replicate) and a global online buyer network that generates the best returns for insurers. It has founder-led, conservative capital allocation (net cash, almost no M&A) and a secular tailwind from rising total-loss frequency. The 2026 slowdown is testing whether the moat converts into growth.

## 3. What the market is doubting — why 16.6x
The shares fell from $37.65 in Feb-2026 (SA headline) to $27.51 (FactSet close). They traded far higher, at a much richer multiple, in 2024–25 (from memory; not re-verified here). Five doubts: (1) **US insurance volume is falling.** Consumers are dropping collision coverage, collision claim frequency is down 3.4% y/y (CEO, 10-Sep-2026), and Copart lost one large insurer account (CEO, 10-Sep-2026; we assume it went to the competitor, an estimate). (2) **FY2026 EPS fell** (−2.5%, the first decline in the decade) as margins compressed to 35.4%. (3) **ADAS/autonomy** threatens accident frequency over the long term. (4) **Interest income** of roughly $200m pre-tax (estimate) shrinks as rates fall. (5) A premium multiple de-rated toward a mid-teens "normal" once growth slowed (estimate).
Our view: (1) is **largely cyclical**. Coverage shedding follows the 2023–25 premium spike and reverses as premiums fall. The customer loss is **real** but a single event, and international and non-insurance volumes are growing. (3) is **right but slow** over five years: total-loss frequency has risen faster than accident frequency has fallen for decades. (2) and (4) are **right**. The 10-year 19% EPS CAGR will not repeat, and ~8% is the realistic algorithm.

## 4. TEST 2 — P(FY2032 EPS ≥ 1.30 × FY1 = $2.11)
FY1 (FY2027) $1.62 × 1.30 = $2.11. FY3 (FY2029) consensus is $1.91 (FactSet, n=3), so ~3.4%/yr is needed in FY2030–32. The net cash ($4.4bn) is a backstop: deploying it into buybacks at a ~6% earnings yield vs a ~4% cash yield is accretive.

| Threat | Prob. (5y) | EPS impact | Contribution to failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structural US insurance volume decline (coverage shedding persists, further share loss to IAA) | 20% | growth falls to ~2–4% | ~8% |
| ADAS/autonomy cuts accident frequency faster than total-loss frequency rises | 10% | −3 to −5 pts growth | ~4% |
| Recession or used-car price collapse in FY2031–32 | 15% | −10 to −15% | ~4% |
| Rate cuts erase interest income | 30% | −3 to −5% | ~3% |
| Land/capex intensity keeps compressing margin | 10% | −5% | ~2% |

Failure ≈ 8 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 2 = 21%, plus 1 for unknowns, less ~0 for overlap → **P(Test 2) ≈ 78%**. FAIL. This is consistent with good franchises carrying a credible volume threat (CARG/PLNT 75%, ATMU 78%). The net cash keeps it above those peers despite the weaker near-term consensus.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS +≥7% in every year FY2027–FY2031)
History: 9/10 years ≥7% (FactSet), including FY2020 (+14.2%). Salvage volumes are fairly recession-resistant (accidents happen in any economy), but the FY2026 decline shows EPS is not immune to insurance-cycle and used-car price swings.
- FY2027: consensus +4.5% (high $1.67 = +7.7%) → P(≥7%) ≈ 0.15
- P(each later year ≥7%) ≈ 0.75; P(recession in FY28–31) 0.45 with P(≥7% that year) 0.5
- 0.15 × 0.75³ × (0.55×0.75 + 0.45×0.5) = 0.15 × 0.42 × 0.64 = 0.04
- **P(Test 3) ≈ 4%**. FAIL. FY1 consensus breaks the test.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key question is whether US insurance unit volumes return to growth as auto premiums normalise and consumers restore collision coverage, while Copart holds share against IAA. Evidence that would raise P(Test 2) above 85%: two quarters of positive domestic insurance units, EBIT margin stabilising at ≥36%, and continued deployment of the cash pile into buybacks. A negative change would be another large insurer moving to IAA.

## 7. Earnings quality
**Flag (FCF conversion).** No adjusted/GAAP issue: the gap is −0.6% 5y avg (FactSet), and GAAP equals adjusted. FCF conversion is 0.70x 10y / 0.76x 5y (FactSet), below 80%, because of heavy land purchases (capex $569m FY2025 and $337m FY2026, FactSet). That spending builds an owned, appreciating asset that is the moat, not maintenance capex. Normalised for growth land, conversion is ~0.9x (estimate). Interest income on cash (estimate ~$200m pre-tax) is a non-operating part of EPS that is sensitive to rates.
