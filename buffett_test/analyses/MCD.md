# McDonald's (MCD-US) — Buffett driver analysis
As-of 2026-10-19 | NTM P/E 17.8 (FactSet, 19-Oct-2026) | Mkt cap $171.9bn (FactSet, 19-Oct-2026) | Test 1 (v2) route: PE (pe_route=Y, fcf_route=N) | FCF yield 4.6% (NTM consensus FCFPS $11.15 / price, FactSet; LTM 4.1%) | RBICS Hospitality Services / Restaurants and Bars | Confidence: Medium
Specialness (PM Part 1 test): **Good–track for dislocation**. McDonald's has a compelling customer value proposition, unmatched global scale, and a royalty-plus-rent model on owned real estate (46% EBIT margin) that is close to a perpetual toll. But the moat is not widening, there is no secular growth driver (US traffic is flat to negative), and growth now depends on unit openings and price. It is a superb business but a ~6% EPS grower.

## 3. What the market is doubting — why 17.8x (from a long-run mid-20s multiple)
The shares fell from ~$306 (Nov-2025) through $280 (May-2026) and $269 (Aug-2026) to $243 (SA headlines; FactSet close).
- **US comps.** Q2 FY26 US comps were +0.8% and July turned slightly negative. McValue 2.0 "has not had the desired impact" (SA, 4-Aug-2026).
- **Low-income consumer.** Low-income visits across QSR fell by double digits.
- **Leadership.** The US president was replaced unexpectedly (SA, 4-Aug-2026).
- **Franchisee economics.** Pressure on franchisee margins and construction costs may slow unit growth from 2027 (RBC, SA 8-May-2026).
- **Capex.** Capital spending is rising for the "NEXT" plan ($3.4bn in 2025 vs $1.9bn in 2022, FactSet).
- **GLP-1** and cost-of-living pressure on fast-food frequency.
Our view: the traffic doubts are partly **cyclical** (value wars, a weak low-income consumer). But the deeper doubt, that McDonald's is a mid-single-digit EPS grower and not the 10%+ grower of 2021–23, is **right**. The 2021–23 surge came from post-COVID recovery and margin gains that are now at a 10-year high. At ~6% growth plus a ~2.4% dividend yield (estimate), 18x is fair rather than cheap.

Failure ≈ 17 + 4 + 4 + 2 + 3 = 30%, less ~0 for overlap → **P(Test 2) ≈ 70%**. FAIL. This puts MCD in line with AOS (70%, driver stack 6.0) and below ATMU/BRC (75–78%). The business is extremely durable, but the growth rate is too close to the bar.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS +≥7% in every year FY2027–FY2031)
History: only 6/10 years ≥7% (FactSet). The misses were 2019 (−0.8%), 2020 (−22.8%), 2024 (−1.8%) and 2025 (+4.1%). Consensus FY2028 is +5.0%. The royalty/rent stream is recurring, but EPS is sensitive to traffic, FX and margin.
- FY2027: consensus +7.0% (low $12.81 = −0.6%) → P(≥7%) ≈ 0.45
- FY2028: consensus +5.0% → P(≥7%) ≈ 0.25; later years ≈ 0.45 each
- 0.45 × 0.25 × 0.45³ = 0.010; allowance for buyback smoothing and correlated good years → 0.02
- **P(Test 3) ≈ 2%**. FAIL.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key question is whether "McDonald's NEXT" (one app, one loyalty program, AI-enabled data, record unit openings) turns US traffic positive and delivers durable comps of ~3–4% with flat-to-rising margins. That combination would lift EPS growth to ~7–8% and P(Test 2) toward 85%. A negative change would be unit growth falling below 3% as franchisee returns compress, or US comps staying negative into 2027.
