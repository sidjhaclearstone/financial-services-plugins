Specialness (PM Part 1 test): **Special (with an AI caveat)**. Three attributes apply:
- **Compelling customer value proposition.** AutoCAD, Revit and Civil 3D are the AEC standards, with file-format, skills and code-compliance lock-in.
- **Secular growth.** Construction, infrastructure and data-centre digitisation.
- **A moat that is arguably widening.** Cloud (ACC, Forma, Fusion), direct customer relationships under the new transaction model, and design data that AI models need.

Heavy SBC and the risk of AI disruption keep it from being clear-cut.

## 3. What the market is doubting (why <18x / 5.9% FCF yield)
The stock is $240.70, down from $317.67 in Oct-2025 (StreetAccount), about −24%. At 17.5x adjusted EPS it looks cheap. GAAP EPS is about half of adjusted ($5.23 vs $10.43, FactSet), so the multiple on GAAP earnings is about 35x. The market doubts four things:
- **The generic AI threat to seat-based application software.** If agentic and generative design reduces the number of drafters, seats decline.
- **Execution risk** from the sales restructuring and the new transaction model. Management itself flagged "near-term caution due to sales force transition disruptions" (Mar-2026).
- **SBC dilution** that buybacks only offset.
- **Governance hangover** from the 2024 audit-committee review of FCF and margin practices and the activist involvement (estimate, public record).

Assessment: **mostly overdone on AI.** Autodesk owns the design data, file formats and compliance workflows that AI needs, and is monetising through consumption. Management argues it is "years ahead… in AI" because direct relationships and consumption billing let it do AI "without blowing a hole in our margins" (IR, Dec-2025). Customer spending is "resilient… supported by persistent project backlogs" (Mar-2026). The doubt is **right on SBC**. Owner FCF yield after SBC is about 4.5% (estimate), not 5.9%.

## 4. TEST 2 — P(EPS FYE Jan-2032 ≥ 1.30 × FY1 consensus)
The bar is 1.30 × $12.59 = **$16.37**. FY3 consensus is already $16.18 (low $12.77), so FY3–FY6 needs only ~0.4%/yr. A 10%/yr path from FY3 gives $21.5, a 31% cushion.

Arithmetic: Σ ≈ 15.2%, plus ~3% for unknowns ⇒ P(fail) ≈ 18% ⇒ **P(Test2) ≈ 82% → FAIL.** This is set just below ADBE (85%). The disruption threat is the same, but ADSK's record is shorter (7/9 years) and it has less buyback firepower.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS ≥ +7% in each of next 5 years)
- History: **7/9** years at ≥7% (FactSet). The misses were during the subscription transition (FY2016–17). Since then EPS has grown every year, including FYE Jan-2021 (+25%). Revenue is ~97% recurring (estimate).
- FY1 (FYE Jan-2027, two quarters reported): +20.7%, low +20.1% ⇒ 0.98.
- FY2: +12.5%, low $13.19 = +4.7% ⇒ 0.85.
- FY3: +14.2%, low −9.8% ⇒ 0.78. FY4–5 ⇒ 0.75 each, given seat cuts in a construction downturn (as in 2009) or AI-pricing transition years.
- P ≈ 0.98 × 0.85 × 0.78 × 0.75 × 0.75 ≈ 0.37. Haircut ×0.85 for recession seat cuts ⇒ **≈ 30% → FAIL.**

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key question is whether AI raises Autodesk's revenue per customer or lowers its seat count. Three things would lift Test 2 toward 88% and Test 3 toward 38%: consumption/AI revenue becoming visible (>5% of revenue by FY2029), net revenue retention stable or rising, and SBC falling below 10% of revenue. Two things would push Test 2 toward 70%: seat declines in AEC, or a second year of billings disruption from the go-to-market change.

## 7. Earnings quality
**Flag (ADJ_GAAP_GAP).** The adjusted vs GAAP gap averages 89.6% over 5y and was 99.5% in FY2025 (FactSet). The causes are stock-based compensation (~$0.7bn/yr, estimate), amortisation of acquired intangibles, and restructuring. FCF conversion of 1.93 over 5y (FactSet) is boosted by upfront annual billing, deferred revenue and the SBC add-back. On adjusted net income it is about 1.06 (FY2025: $2.38bn ÷ ~$2.24bn). Count SBC as a real cost; it is netted off in §2.
