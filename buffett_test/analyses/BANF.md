## 3. What the market is doubting
13.5x for a bank with a 13% 10y EPS CAGR looks cheap, but EPS is at a 10y high after the 2021–25 NIM tailwind and the few analysts covering it see only 2–4% growth beyond FY2026. Management itself calls the longer-term outlook "a mixed bag" and is keeping a "healthy" credit-loss allowance (Q1-26 release). Oklahoma energy-loan exposure adds cyclicality. The doubt is largely right on growth; it may be wrong on downside (credit record is excellent and the balance sheet is overcapitalised).

## 4. TEST 2 — P(EPS FY2031 ≥ 1.30 × 7.72 = $10.04)
Consensus FY3 $8.22 would require 6.9%/yr for FY2029–31 — the test requires an acceleration nobody forecasts.
| Threat | P | EPS impact |
|---|---|---|
| Rate-cut cycle compresses NIM | 25% | −10–15% |
| Energy/agri credit cycle in Oklahoma | 20% | −10–20% (1–2 years) |
| Growth stays at consensus 2–4% (no catalyst) | 40% | path ends ~$9.0–9.5 (fails alone) |
| Capital left idle (no buyback/M&A) | — | embedded above |
Base-case path at consensus (≈3%/yr after FY1) gives ~$8.9 in FY2031 — a fail. Passing needs ~5.4%/yr, achievable only with redeployment of excess capital (buybacks/M&A) or a sustained higher-rate regime. P(pass) ≈ P(growth ≥5.4% absent shocks) 0.55 × P(no major rate/credit shock) 0.65 + partial credit ≈ 0.36 + 0.04 = **P(Test2) ≈ 40% → FAIL.**

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS ≥ 7% in EVERY one of next 5 years)
History 7/10 years ≥7% (FactSet), but −26% in 2020 and +1.6% in 2024; spread income is not recurring in the Test-3 sense. Consensus FY2 +4.1% and FY3 +2.2% already break the chain. P ≈ 0.25 × 0.20 × 0.5^3 ≈ 0.6%; with estimate error → **P(Test3) ≈ 2% → FAIL.**

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
Capital allocation of a growing surplus: a large accretive acquisition or a sustained buyback programme (~2–3% of shares p.a.) would add 2–3 pts of EPS growth and could push P(Test2) above 70%. Consensus is very thin (n=1–3), so estimates may be stale.
