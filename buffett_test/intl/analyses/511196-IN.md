## 3. What the market is doubting
At 8.1x NTM, about 1.5x book (estimate), the market applies four discounts:
(a) **Monoline HFC.** Large banks (SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI) dominate salaried home loans with cheaper deposits and can squeeze spreads at will.
(b) **PSU parentage.** Canara Bank is promoter (~30%, estimate). Management rotates, Canara may monetise its stake, and decisions are slower.
(c) **Governance memory.** The 2023 branch fraud (triage note).
(d) **A near-term slowdown**: FY27 consensus +3.7%, with GNPA up in H1 FY27.

The doubt is **partly right**. The franchise is real: EPS rose in 9 of 10 years, and the only decline was −2.4% in FY19 through the IL&FS funding shock. But the moat is thin, and FY26 was a peak-spread year. The state-ownership discount is valid but modest, since the company is run commercially.

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS ≥ +7% in each of FY27–FY31)
FY1 (FY27) consensus is +3.7% vs a ₹87.25 threshold (std ₹2.35, high ₹90.0). z ≈ 1.15 → **P(FY27 ≥7%) ≈ 0.13.** History: 8/10 years ≥7% (misses: FY19 −2.4%, FY22 +3.6%). Housing loans are long-tenor and recurring, but lender EPS depends on spread and credit cost.
P ≈ 0.13 × 0.75^4 × 0.85 ≈ **0.035 → P(Test 3) ≈ 3%. FAIL.** This is consistent with banks rarely exceeding 25%, and the weak FY1 decides it.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is whether the spread holds at ≥2.75% while banks push salaried home loans, i.e. whether FY26 was a peak or a new base. If FY27 delivers the guided 13–14% AUM growth with NIM at ≥3.75% and GNPA falls in H2, P(Test 2) rises to ~80%. A Canara stake sale to a weaker owner, or spread compression below 2.5%, would cut it to ~60%. FX: EPS is in INR; INR depreciation (historically ~3%/yr, estimate) lowers USD returns.
