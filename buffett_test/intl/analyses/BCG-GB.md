## 3. What the market is doubting
At 16x, the stock is well below its IPO-era 30x+ (estimate). The market doubts four things:
- **(a) Growth.** Revenue growth slowed to 7% in Apr-26, and inventory headwinds hit Lithuania real estate and Estonian autos.
- **(b) Pricing headroom.** Management is lighter on Estonian auto pricing because of "dealer price sensitivity" (StreetAccount Q&A, 2-Jul-2026).
- **(c) Geopolitics.** The Baltics sit on NATO's eastern flank, and investors apply a frontier-risk discount.
- **(d) Liquidity.** It is a small-cap LSE listing with a non-UK business.

The country/geopolitical discount is real and partly rational. The business doubt is mostly temporary: competitive positions remain robust ("Autoplius maintains a significant lead", same source).

## 5. TEST 3 — P(EPS ≥ +7% in each of FY Apr-28 to Apr-32)
- Revenue is mostly recurring (dealer/agent subscriptions plus listing fees).
- Pricing power is demonstrated, but volume is cyclical (car and property transactions).
- In a weak year, buybacks add about 3 pts.
- History: 3 of 3 years at ≥7%, but this is too short to include a recession. FY Apr-26 was +11% on only +7% revenue.

P(no Baltic downturn in 5 years) ≈ 0.40 × P(each normal year ≥7%) ≈ 0.88^5 ≈ 0.53 → ≈21%, rounded to **≈18%** because the record is short. **FAIL.**

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is how much pricing headroom remains before agents or dealers push back or regulators intervene, in a market with almost no volume growth. Five more years of pricing at 7–9% with stable volumes through a downturn would lift P(Test2) to 85% or more. FX translation: BCG reports in EUR, with all revenue from the Baltic eurozone and an LSE listing in GBP. There is no operating translation risk; a USD investor carries EUR/USD exposure.

## 7. Earnings quality
The adjusted-vs-GAAP gap averaged 24.1% over 5 years, which is **>15%: flag**. It comes from amortisation of acquired intangibles from the 2019 Apax-led carve-out, IPO costs and share-based payment (PSP). The latest gap is 10.7% and shrinking. FCF conversion of 1.61x is genuine, because amortisation is non-cash and capex is about 0.7% of revenue.
