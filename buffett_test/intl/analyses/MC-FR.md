## 3. What the market is doubting
LVMH trades at 16.5x NTM with a 6.6% FCF yield. Both are at the low end of its own history (estimate; it traded mostly in the 20s in 2017–23). The doubts:
(1) A **multi-year luxury down-cycle**: EPS −17% in 2024 and −11% in 2025, flat in 2026 (FactSet), with Chinese aspirational demand weak.
(2) **Price elasticity** after aggressive 2021–24 price rises.
(3) **Tariffs and cognac**: China anti-dumping action and US tariffs hit Hennessy.
(4) **FX translation** from a weaker USD and Asian currencies.
(5) **Succession** (Bernard Arnault is 77), plus a **French tax/political** overhang (Arnault cited "policies… to tax [companies] to the hilt"; Q4-25 call).
(6) A **controlled-company discount**: the Arnault family holds ~49% of capital and ~64% of votes (estimate, public filings; not verified here).
On the evidence, (1) is cyclical and largely temporary: the brands kept ~22% EBIT margins at the trough (FactSet), and FCF rose in 2025. (2) and (3) are partly right and have reset the growth algorithm lower. There is no state ownership.

## 6. Key uncertainty and what would change the view
The key uncertainty is whether 2024–26 is a cyclical trough, or a reset to ~5% organic growth as price-led growth exhausts itself. Two things would lift P(Test 2) to ~78–80%: Fashion & Leather organic growth back to high single digits in 2027 with margin recovery toward 24%. A second year of FY2/FY3 downgrades would cut it to ~60–65%; 8 of 14 FCF estimates were cut in the last 100 days (FactSet).
**FX:** EPS is in EUR, while revenue is global (US ~25%, Asia ~30%; estimate). A weaker USD/CNY hurts translation, which management puts at ~70 bp of H2-26 margin. A USD investor also bears EUR/USD on the equity.
